As we enter July, the night sky offers some excellent opportunities for amateur astronomers and casual stargazers alike. With Florida's clear skies and minimal light pollution in many areas, this month provides a wonderful chance to observe some of the most spectacular celestial events.

The first major event of the month is the Perseid meteor shower, which reaches its peak around July 17th. While the meteors are visible throughout the month, the peak night offers the best chance to see up to 100 meteors per hour. The meteors appear to radiate from the constellation Perseus, which is visible in the northeastern sky after midnight.

For those with telescopes, Jupiter and Saturn will be particularly prominent in the evening sky. Both planets are well-positioned for observation, with Jupiter's Great Red Spot and Saturn's rings clearly visible under good conditions. Mars, while less prominent, will also be visible in the western sky.

July is also an excellent month for observing the Andromeda Galaxy, which is visible in the northeastern sky. This is one of the largest galaxies visible to the naked eye, and can be easily located with basic star charts.

![A view of the night sky with meteor shower](2026-07-15-header.jpg "Celestial events in July")

A particularly exciting opportunity is the appearance of Comet 45P/Honda-Mrkos-Pajdušáková, which will be visible in the morning sky around the 20th. This comet's path brings it close to the moon's position, making it easier to locate.

The astronomical calendar for July also includes several planetary conjunctions, where planets appear very close together in the sky. These events are particularly beautiful for photography and provide excellent opportunities for amateur astronomers to observe planetary positions.

For Florida residents, the timing of these events makes them particularly accessible. The long summer nights and clear skies allow for extended observation periods, especially for those who prefer to observe before dawn when the skies are often darkest.

For those looking to enhance their stargazing experience, I recommend bringing a star chart, a red flashlight to preserve night vision, and comfortable seating. The key to successful amateur astronomy is patience and good observing conditions.

### July Observing Schedule

- **July 17**: Peak Perseid meteor shower (100+ meteors/hour)
- **July 20**: Comet 45P/Honda-Mrkos-Pajdušáková visible
- **July 22**: Venus and Jupiter conjunction
- **July 28**: Saturn's rings in peak visibility

July provides an excellent opportunity for amateur astronomers to observe some of the most spectacular celestial events. Whether you're a seasoned observer or just starting your astronomy journey, this month offers something for everyone.
