I've always been fascinated by the challenge of making vintage computers work in today's digital world. Recently, I took on the project of refurbishing an old Compaq Portable computer, which had been sitting in my garage for years.

The process began with a thorough cleaning and inspection. The computer's case was grimy, and I had to carefully remove the keyboard and clean it with compressed air. The screen was dark, so I had to replace the backlight. This was a delicate process that required understanding the internal components and how they connected.

The next challenge was updating the operating system. I chose to install a minimal Linux distribution that would run efficiently on the system's limited resources. The process required careful configuration and optimization to ensure that the system could handle modern tasks.

One of the most rewarding aspects was the hands-on experience with hardware components. I learned how to identify faulty components, replace capacitors, and troubleshoot power supply issues. This process deepened my appreciation for the engineering that went into vintage systems and the importance of hardware craftsmanship.

![A restored vintage computer with clean case and screen](2026-06-01-header.jpg "Restored vintage computer")

## Restoration Tips

- Clean hardware thoroughly before starting
- Identify and replace faulty components
- Use lightweight operating systems for old hardware
- Test components carefully to avoid further damage

This project reinforced my belief in the value of hardware restoration and the importance of maintaining and reusing older technology.
